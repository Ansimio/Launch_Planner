What the model actually does

  • One SKU, one print per order. A4 giclée, unframed. Both options use the same supplier's published price tiers, so a cheaper photo print is not confused with a fine-art print.
  • Batch: buy the whole quantity before selling, pay one inbound delivery, and fulfil up to stock available.
  • After order: buy each print individually, receive it yourself, then pack and post it. This is not supplier-direct dropshipping. Each order incurs an assumed inbound delivery charge.
  • Packaging: purchase in lots of 25 sets. £0.60 per set is an allowance for envelope and protection, not a quoted all-in price. Packaging delivery is a separate editable input.
  • Contribution: receipts − cost of sold prints − inbound delivery − consumed packaging − postage − payment fees − contingency reserve − fixed launch costs − owner time.
  • Cash change after reserve: receipts − all cash purchases and costs − reserve. Unsold stock and unused packaging tie up cash but remain assets at cost.
  • Break-even: first whole-order count with nonnegative contribution. Batch is capped at available stock; after-order search is capped at 500 orders.
  • Reserve: a user-defined percentage of receipts for refunds, damage or other uncertainty. It is not a researched return rate or a full returns model.
  • Time: owner hours are an imputed cost, not payroll or an additional cash payment. All financial computations use pennies, rounding each order's fees and reserve.
Model scope: UK direct sales, standard UK cards, a seller not reclaiming input VAT, no output VAT in this scenario. No marketplace commissions. Taxes, ongoing overhead, customer-payment timing, salvage/write-downs and repeat purchases are excluded. Do not read contribution as net profit.
